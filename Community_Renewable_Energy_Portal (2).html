Renewable energy basics

Solar

Rooftop panels turn sunlight into electricity. India gets 4–5 peak sun hours a day in most regions.

Wind

Turbines suit open, windy sites. Small turbines can add to rooftop solar in coastal and hilly areas.

Biomass and biogas

Kitchen, farm and garden waste can produce cooking gas and power.

Hydro

Flowing water drives generators. Mostly large-scale, with small micro-hydro in some villages.

India’s clean power today

Non-fossil power capacity reached 300.5 GW on 31 July 2026. That is over 60% of India’s 500 GW target for 2030, and more than 54% of the country’s roughly 552 GW total. India added a record 55.29 GW in 2025–26, and ranks third in the world for renewable capacity.

Source: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, as of 31 July 2026. Capacity is not the same as generation: non-fossil sources supplied 29.2% of India’s electricity in 2025–26, so there is still a long way to go.

Myths and facts

Solar does not work on cloudy days.

Panels still produce power under clouds, just less. Monthly output averages out over the year.

Solar is too expensive for ordinary homes.

Central and state subsidies, plus bank loans, cut the upfront cost. Most homes recover the cost in several years.

Tenants can claim the solar subsidy.

PM Surya Ghar is meant for people who own the house. A tenant cannot claim it alone, but can ask the owner to apply or join a community installation.

Any panel qualifies for the subsidy.

Only panels on the government’s approved list (ALMM) qualify, so use a registered vendor.

Panels need constant maintenance.

Cleaning every few weeks and an annual inspection is usually enough.

Go solar and you will never pay an electricity bill.

Net metering offsets your units, but fixed charges and night-time use may still appear on the bill.

How rooftop solar works

  1. Sunlight falls on the panels on your roof and they produce direct current (DC) electricity.
  2. An inverter changes it to alternating current (AC), the kind your home appliances use.
  3. Your home uses the solar power first. Any extra goes to the grid through a net meter.
  4. The meter records what you send out and what you draw in, and your bill is reduced by the difference.

Why it matters

For your household

Lower electricity bills for years, and protection from future tariff rises. Use the savings calculator to see your own numbers.

For the community

Shared solar for common areas cuts society maintenance costs. Local installation and servicing also create jobs. PM Surya Ghar has reported about 38,500 direct jobs.

For the environment

Solar produces no smoke or fumes while generating power. Each unit of solar replaces a unit made from coal, which cuts CO₂ and air pollution. India aims for net zero emissions by 2070.

Did you know?

  • India’s solar capacity grew from about 2.8 GW in 2014 to 164.59 GW in July 2026.
  • India installed a record 44.6 GW of solar in 2025–26 alone.
  • A 1 kW rooftop system gives roughly 4 units a day (about 120 a month), depending on sunlight.
  • Solar panels typically keep working for 25 years or more with basic cleaning.
  • More than 1.4 crore diesel pumps run on Indian farms, burning about 6 billion litres of diesel a year. Solar pumps under PM-KUSUM replace them.
  • Switching to LED bulbs is the cheapest way to start saving energy today.

Sources: MNRE and PIB statements and 2026 news reports. Pump figures are approximate.

How to get rooftop solar with the subsidy

Steps for PM Surya Ghar

  1. Register on the national portal, pmsuryaghar.gov.in. Choose your state and electricity company (DISCOM) and enter your consumer number and mobile number.
  2. Apply for a rooftop solar system and wait for your DISCOM’s approval.
  3. Have it installed by a registered vendor, using panels from the ALMM approved list.
  4. Get the net meter fitted and the system inspected. The DISCOM issues a commissioning report.
  5. Upload the report with your bank details. The subsidy arrives in your bank account in about 30 days.

The whole process usually takes 2 to 4 months. You pay the full cost first and receive the subsidy afterwards. Solar loans exist: one published example is about ₹1,098 a month for a ₹1 lakh loan over 10 years at 5.75%. Rates change, so ask your bank. Helpline: 15555.

Save energy first

  • Switch to LED bulbs and star-rated appliances.
  • Set the AC to 24–26 °C and use a fan with it.
  • Unplug chargers and switch off standby devices.
  • Use a solar water heater instead of an electric geyser.
  • Smaller bills also mean a smaller, cheaper solar system.
